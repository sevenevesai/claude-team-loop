# Write an update

The reader is busy, often not technical, and will judge the team by this text. Give them the
answer, what it means for them, and what happens next.

## 1. Know the reader

Work out from the request, the thread being answered and the project's context file: who reads
this (a customer, a stakeholder, a colleague, a vendor), what they asked or need to decide, which
channel it goes to, and which language the thread is in. When the reader or the purpose is
unclear, ask one question before drafting.

## 2. Collect the facts

Take every fact from this session's work, the project records, or a check you run now. Every
number must be traceable to a query, a file or a record; the sources go in the note under the
draft (step 4), not in the message. Leave out what you could not verify, or mark it as expected
and not yet confirmed. Do not soften a problem or enlarge a result.

## 3. Draft

- Lead with the answer or the outcome. Then what it means for the reader. Then what you need
  from them or what happens next, with a date when one is known.
- Leave out how it was done unless the reader is technical or asked.
- Length follows the channel: a chat message or stand-up line, up to five lines; an email, up
  to about 150 words; a ticket comment, the facts and the next step.
- Answer a list of questions point by point, in their order.
- Write in the language of the thread. Match the tone and layout the reader's side already
  uses when an example is in front of you.
- Use plain verbs and concrete nouns. No openers that restate the question, no praise, no
  stacked hedges, no "seamless", "robust", "leverage", "ensure" as filler. Write as the person
  would to someone they work with every week.

## 4. Show it

Present the draft ready to copy. Underneath, for the person only: where each number came from,
anything unverified, and any choice you made for them (for example, a date you proposed).

Gate: send, post or comment only after the person says so, and name the exact place it will
go (which ticket, which channel, which recipients) when you ask.

## 5. Afterwards

When the message commits the team to something (a date, a fix, a follow-up), add it to the open
items in the project's change record, or mention it for `close-out`.

## Ask the person only for

- The reader and purpose, when the request does not show them.
- Commitments: dates, scope, prices and apologies are theirs to make.

## Local practice

Before starting, look for `.claude/team-overrides.md` in the working folder, or a project
knowledge file named `team-overrides.md`. Follow its `all` section and its section for this skill
over the defaults here. It cannot waive a step marked Gate.
